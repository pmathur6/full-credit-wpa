The idea

Win Probability Added (WPA) measures how much each play changed a team's chance of winning. Traditional WPA gives the whole change to the batter and the pitcher. If a shortstop dives to rob a hit, the pitcher gets the credit; if a left fielder misplays a routine fly, the pitcher takes the blame.

Full-Credit WPA splits every play across everyone who took part: the batter, the runners, the pitcher, the catcher, the fielders and the team's coaching staff. Season totals add up those shares, so a great defender or baserunner finally shows up next to the big bats and aces.

The rules

  1. Results, not expectations. Every play is scored on what actually happened. The batting team gets the real change in win probability and the fielding team gets exactly the opposite, so every play, and every game, adds to zero. Players eat their own bad luck: a line drive caught at the wall is still an out for the batter.
  2. Statcast only decides the split. Expected values (expected batting average, outfield catch probability, our infield model, typical runner advancement) are used to divide each side's actual share among its players. They never add or take away credit.
  3. Defense on a ball in play is split in stages.
    • Contact goes to the pitcher: how dangerous was the ball he allowed, against an average defense?
    • Positioning goes to coaching: how much easier or harder did the fielders' starting spots make it?
    • Conversion goes to the fielder: did he make the play, given how hard it was from where he stood?
    • Type of hit or out (single vs double, one out vs two) goes back to the pitcher, since it mostly reflects where and how the ball was hit.
    • Runner advancement on the play goes to the throwers who cut a runner down, or to the fielder who fielded the ball.
  4. Offense with runners on: the batter gets his result at standard advancement; each runner gets credit or blame for taking more or fewer bases than usual.
  5. Events between pitches (steals, pickoffs, wild pitches, passed balls, balks) are their own plays, measured at the count when they happened.

The buckets

Every player credit lands in one of four buckets, shown in the same colors across the site:

  • Batting: the batter's share of each plate appearance, at standard runner advancement.
  • Baserunning: steals, extra bases, tagging up and advancing on wild pitches (positive); caught stealing, pickoffs, being thrown out or doubled off (negative). Includes the batter's own trip around the bases after a hit.
  • Fielding: catches and misses, throws that cut down runners, errors, and catcher work on steals and passed balls. One number per player, combined across every position he played.
  • Pitching: the pitcher's share of each plate appearance, plus wild pitches, balks and pickoffs, and his effect on would-be base stealers.

For pitchers, the site also shows defense behind him: what his fielders and coaching added or cost on his plays. His pitching, his own fielding and the defense behind him always add up to his traditional WPA.

Coaching

A team-level bucket for decisions that changed plays. It isn't meant to judge managers' pinch-hitting or bullpen moves; it captures the coaching staff's influence on individual plays.

  • Defensive positioning. Outfield: Statcast's catch probability from the fielder's actual starting spot, compared with the average for balls hit like this one. Infield: our model of how often an average infield converts a ground ball, with and without the alignment the team used, then calibrated so each infielder's total matches Statcast's Outs Above Average.
  • Intentional walks. The defense's whole share goes to coaching; the batter keeps his.
  • Sacrifice bunt calls, credited to the batting team's coaching.
  • Defensive indifference: letting a runner take a base unchallenged.

Wins vs runs

The Wins view weights every play by the game situation: a two-out hit in a tie game in the 9th can be worth many times more than the same hit in a blowout. That's what actually decided games, but it's noisy and depends on opportunity.

The Runs (context-neutral) view runs the exact same split on run expectancy: how many runs a team expects to score from each base, out and count situation, built from that season's games. Score and inning don't matter, so every play counts the same. Traditional run value (RE24) is the comparison column there. Runs are better for judging skill; wins for judging impact.

The same player can look different in the two views. A fielder who makes a routine-looking play in a tie game late gets more wins than runs; a player who piles up value in lopsided games gets more runs than wins.

Comparing with WAR

WAR and this site measure from different zeros. WPA, ours included, compares a player with an average player and, in the Wins view, counts when things happened. WAR compares with a replacement-level player (roughly a AAA call-up), ignores game situation, and adjusts for position.

Tick WAR scale on the leaderboard to add a column on WAR's footing. It starts from the Runs view, which already ignores game situation, and:

  • re-centers each bucket on the league average (as WAR's components are);
  • adds replacement level: about 18 runs per 600 PA for hitters and 16 per 700 batters faced for pitchers, which gives the usual 1,000 WAR per season, 570 to position players and 430 to pitchers;
  • adds FanGraphs' positional adjustments (catcher +12.5 runs a season down to DH −17.5), pro-rated by time at each position, with a league adjustment so they net to zero;
  • divides by runs per win for that season (about 9.8 in 2026).

Pete Crow-Armstrong's 2026: +77 runs above average, plus 22 for replacement level and 4 for playing center field, comes to about 10.8 wins, in line with his published WAR. His Wins number (+6.4) is lower because less of that value came in high-leverage moments. The WAR scale has no park adjustment and uses one replacement level for starters and relievers, so expect small differences from FanGraphs and Baseball-Reference, which also differ from each other.

How we check it

  • Every play balances. The batting team's credits equal the play's change in win probability, and the fielding team's equal the opposite, for all ~190,000 plays a season, to rounding error. Each team's season total equals its wins minus its summed starting win probability.
  • Our play-by-play matches MLB's. Bases, outs and score are tracked independently and match Statcast at the start of 100% of plate appearances.
  • Against public stats (2026):

    Batting vs traditional batter WPA0.98

    Batting and pitching track traditional WPA closely by design, minus what we hand to runners and fielders. Fielding and baserunning agree with Statcast's run values but aren't identical, because ours are weighted by game situation.
  • Signal vs noise. Splitting each season into alternating games, baserunning and fielding become reliable faster than batting does in wins: a season of win-weighted batting is about as much luck as skill. Outfield outs above expected stabilize in about 35 games.

Limitations

  • Infield line drives and popups have no public catch probability, so the pitcher keeps the whole play. The fielder who caught it is still shown, with negligible credit.
  • Turning double plays is credited to the pitcher for now; a model for the pivot and relay is planned.
  • Fielder jumps vs positioning. Outfield catch probability is measured from where the fielder started, so his jump and route are his, and his starting spot is coaching. A fielder who positions himself well gets that credited to coaching.
  • No pitch framing, by design. Credit is assigned on what happens to the plate appearance (and the events between pitches), not pitch by pitch, so a stolen strike shows up only through the PA's result, which goes to the batter and pitcher. Catchers who frame well will look lower here than in fWAR, which counts framing.
  • Intentional walks can't be untangled from what happens next, so coaching takes the defense's whole share of the walk itself.
  • Wins are noisy. Like any WPA, this records what happened and how much it mattered, not true talent. Use the runs view for skill.
  • Regular season only, 2023 to the present.

Data

  • Baseball Savant pitch-level data: game state, fielder alignment, batted balls, expected batting average, and MLB's win-probability model.
  • MLB Stats API game feeds: every runner movement with putouts, assists and errors, and events between pitches.
  • Savant outfield catch probability for each play, plus Outs Above Average, catcher throwing, pitcher running game and sprint speed.

Everything runs automatically on GitHub Actions. The code, data checks and reports are in the full-credit-wpa repository.
